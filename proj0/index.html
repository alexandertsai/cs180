Project 0: Becoming Friends with Your Camera

Alexander Tsai

Part 1

Close, no zoom, 24 mm
Far, zoomed in, 227 mm

My hypothesis is that when the camera is much closer, features that differ in distance from the camera, like the nose compared to the cheek, become much more distorted. When the camera is further away, the nose being slightly closer than the cheek matters much less, so there is much less distortion around the face.

Part 2

Far, zoomed in, 53 mm
Close, no zoom, 19 mm

The building appears flattened in the first picture because we are able to capture the whole building from far away. When we get closer, we are trying to maintain the same field of view, but we do not have enough distance to maintain it, so the building looks slightly warped or curvy.
